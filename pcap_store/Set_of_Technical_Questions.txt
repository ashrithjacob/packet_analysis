Below is a sample set of questions you might ask your co-pilot , which already has access to the detailed fields from EAPOL and RADIUS packet captures. Each question is phrased from the perspective of a human analyst who wants insights about the network behavior; the co-pilot has the data, so the question focuses on what the analyst wants to know.

1. Authentication and EAPOL Key Frames
    “Co-pilot, can you verify if the client’s 4-Way Handshake completed successfully for MAC 0E-9D-8F-CD-3B-B6 on SSID ‘VAULT’? If not, which EAPOL Key message or step failed?”
Why This Question?
    An analyst wants a straightforward success/failure assessment of the WPA2/WPA3 4-Way Key exchange, with details about exactly where the process might have stalled.

2. RADIUS vs. Local Authentication
    “Co-pilot, does the RADIUS accounting record for MAC 0E-9D-8F-CD-3B-B6 show Acct-Authentic=RADIUS(1) or Local(2)? And what does that indicate about how this user was authenticated?”
Why This Question?
    Distinguishes whether the device was validated by a RADIUS-based approach (Enterprise or MPSK) or purely local PSK.

3. SSID and AP Correlation
    “Co-pilot, can you confirm that the Called-Station-Id ‘D4-BA-BA-A1-48-43:VAULT’ in the RADIUS logs truly corresponds to the same BSSID and SSID we see in the wireless frames?”
Why This Question?
    Ensures the RADIUS record actually matches the real AP/BSSID on the air, preventing confusion about mismatched logging.

4. VLAN Assignment or Tunnel Attributes
    “Co-pilot, did the RADIUS Access-Accept for this session include any Tunnel-Type=VLAN attributes? If so, what VLAN ID was assigned to the client, and do we see that reflected in subsequent accounting or local logs?”
Why This Question?
    For MPSK or Enterprise setups that do dynamic VLAN assignment, the analyst wants confirmation of the VLAN from the RADIUS packet and that the station was actually placed in it.

5. EAP vs. PSK Determination
    “Co-pilot, from the RSN IE in the EAPOL frames, is the AKM set to PSK(2) or 802.1X(1)? And do we see any EAP-Message attributes in the RADIUS Access-Request? Let’s clarify whether this is a WPA2-Personal, MPSK, or WPA2-Enterprise session.”
Why This Question?
    Helps the analyst quickly identify the authentication method being used.

6. Connect-Info and Link Speed
    “Co-pilot, what does the Connect-Info(77) attribute say about the user’s link speed and PHY?
Why This Question?
    Verifies RADIUS logs (Connect-Info) and actual radio conditions, useful for performance troubleshooting.

7. Session Success vs. Failure Timeline
    “Co-pilot, can you lay out the time sequence from first EAPOL handshake packet to Access-Accept, and then to Acct-Status-Type=Start? If there was a large delay, let me know.”
Why This Question?
    The analyst wants a chronological overview to see if any step took too long, which might cause timeouts.

8. MPSK Real-Time vs. Cached
    “Co-pilot, do we see a RADIUS Access-Request for this client’s MAC every time it connects, or only once after the AP rebooted? I want to confirm if MPSK is done on-the-fly or cached locally.”
Why This Question?
    Helps determine the MPSK vendor approach (real-time RADIUS queries vs. local caching).

9. EAPOL Key Frame Details
    “Co-pilot, in Message #2 of the 4-Way Handshake, does the RSN IE confirm the client and AP both agreed on AES-CCMP, or is there a mismatch? Provide the exact Pairwise Cipher and AKM fields.”
Why This Question?
    Ensures cipher agreement. A mismatch can cause handshake failures.

10. Summarizing the Network Topology
    “Co-pilot, please give me a final summary: for client MAC 0E-9D-8F-CD-3B-B6, SSID ‘VAULT,’ AP BSSID D4-BA-BA-A1-48-43, what was the authentication type (PSK/MPSK/Enterprise), the assigned VLAN (if any), the link speed from Connect-Info, and whether we see an Accounting Start indicating success?”
Why This Question?
    A one-stop question to unify all data points—encryption method, VLAN, speed, success/failure—into a single final insight.
